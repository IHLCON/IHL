Exploring New Business Horizons

Global Business March 12, 2025
Back to Updates

The world of international trade is shifting fast. The United States has introduced new tariffs that are reshaping commercial relationships across the globe — and for businesses in Canada and Europe, this moment presents a significant opportunity to deepen their own partnership under CETA.

A Pivotal Moment for EU-Canada Trade

As businesses face higher costs and mounting trade barriers in the American market, many are turning their attention to the Comprehensive Economic and Trade Agreement (CETA) between the European Union and Canada. The agreement provides a ready-made legal framework for expanding commercial ties — and the current trade climate is accelerating the case for using it.

For companies that have long relied on the United States as a primary market or transit point, the shifting tariff landscape is prompting a strategic reassessment. Those that move quickly to establish or deepen EU-Canada relationships stand to gain a meaningful competitive advantage.

Companies adapting quickly to these market shifts — building EU-Canada relationships now — will gain competitive advantages that may prove durable long after trade policy stabilises.

Key Sectors and Opportunities

Green Energy & Sustainability

Hydrogen energy, battery technologies, and sustainable mining represent significant growth areas. Europe's Green Deal and Canada's net-zero commitments create strong alignment and collaboration potential for businesses in this space.

Technology & Digital Business

Canadian AI and fintech sectors are positioning for European expansion. As the U.S. becomes a less predictable partner, European businesses are increasingly receptive to Canadian technology firms as trusted alternatives.

Aerospace & Manufacturing

Rising U.S. trade barriers are prompting both regions to strengthen partnerships in aviation, defence, and clean transportation — sectors where both Canada and the EU have established industrial depth.

Financial Services

Canada's emergence as a financial hub is attracting European investors, with evolving EU regulations potentially facilitating easier cross-border investment flows in both directions.

Navigating the Compliance Landscape

Pursuing EU-Canada opportunities is not without complexity. Businesses must carefully navigate a layered regulatory environment on both sides of the Atlantic. The EU's General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) establishes strict requirements for data handling that affect any company operating in or serving European markets. On the Canadian side, businesses must understand the country's own privacy regulations and the Foreign Investment Review Process, which governs significant foreign investments in Canada.

These compliance requirements make specialist legal support essential — not just to avoid risk, but to structure transactions, partnerships, and market entry strategies in ways that are both efficient and durable.

The Broader Picture

The current tariff environment represents more than a short-term disruption. It reflects a broader realignment of global trade relationships that is likely to persist in some form regardless of near-term policy changes. Businesses that treat this moment as an opportunity to build diversified international partnerships — rather than simply waiting for stability to return — will be better positioned for the decade ahead.

IHL's international legal and business advisory services are designed precisely for moments like this: helping organisations navigate complex cross-border environments, structure compliant commercial relationships, and move with confidence in markets that are still taking shape.
